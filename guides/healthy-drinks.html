Healthy Drinks for Weight Loss — What to Drink and What to Skip

Drinks can add hundreds of hidden calories. Here is what to choose, what to limit, and how to stay hydrated.

Liquid calories

Drinks are the easiest calories to consume without noticing, because they do not fill you up. A large flavoured coffee can contain 300 calories or more, a bottle of juice can contain as much sugar as a soft drink, and a single alcoholic drink can add 150 to 200 calories. Over a day, drinks can easily add 500 calories without any feeling of fullness. Cutting liquid calories is often the single easiest change in a weight-loss plan, and it requires no willpower at mealtimes, only a change of habit.

Water first

Water is the best drink for weight loss. It has no calories, it supports every process in the body, and drinking a glass before a meal can slightly reduce how much you eat. Some people mistake thirst for hunger, so a glass of water is a good first response to a craving. Sparkling water is a good alternative if you like fizz, and adding lemon, cucumber, or mint gives flavour without calories. Keep a bottle with you so water is the easy choice. The water calculator on our home page gives a rough daily guide of about 35 ml per kilogram of body weight.

Tea and coffee

Unsweetened tea and coffee are nearly calorie-free and can help weight loss: caffeine slightly raises calorie burn and can reduce appetite for a short time. Green tea, black tea, and herbal teas are all good choices. The problem is what gets added: sugar, syrup, and large amounts of milk turn a nearly free drink into a small meal. A black coffee has about 5 calories; a large caramel latte can have 350. If you like milky coffee, use a small amount of milk and skip the sugar and syrup. Avoid energy drinks, which are high in sugar and caffeine.

Juice, smoothies, and soft drinks

Fruit juice, even 100 percent juice with no added sugar, raises blood sugar quickly and lacks the fibre of whole fruit. A glass of orange juice contains the sugar of three or four oranges but none of the fullness. Smoothies are better if they contain whole fruit and vegetables, but they are still easy to over-consume, and shop-bought ones often contain added sugar. Soft drinks, including many sold as healthy, are usually sugary water. Diet drinks are lower in calories, but water and unsweetened tea are better everyday choices.

Alcohol and weight loss

Alcohol adds calories, lowers inhibitions around food, and disrupts sleep, which makes the next day harder. A pint of beer has about 180 calories, a large glass of wine about 190, and a cocktail can have 300 or more. Alcohol also pauses fat burning while the body processes it. You do not need to give up alcohol completely to lose weight, but cutting back helps a lot: choose smaller servings, alternate with water, avoid sugary mixers, and keep some alcohol-free days each week. If you drink most evenings, reducing to weekends alone can save thousands of calories a month.

Building better drink habits

Make the healthy choice the easy choice: keep water visible, keep sugary drinks out of the house, and order water or unsweetened tea when eating out. If you crave something sweet, eat fruit rather than drinking juice. If you drink a lot of coffee with sugar, reduce the sugar gradually over two weeks rather than stopping at once. Track your drinks for a week to see how many calories they add; most people are surprised. Changing drinks is a small change with a large payoff, and it is one of the easiest wins in weight loss.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is fruit juice healthy for weight loss?

Not really. Even 100 percent juice lacks the fibre of whole fruit and raises blood sugar quickly. Eat whole fruit instead and drink water.

Does drinking water help you lose weight?

Yes, modestly. Water has no calories, and a glass before meals can slightly reduce how much you eat. Staying hydrated also helps you tell thirst from hunger.

Can I drink alcohol while losing weight?

Yes, in moderation. Alcohol adds calories and disrupts sleep, so smaller servings, alcohol-free days, and avoiding sugary mixers all help keep your deficit.
