Breaking a Plateau — What to Do When Weight Loss Stops

Why plateaus happen, how to tell a real plateau from normal fluctuation, and the safe steps that restart progress.

What a plateau really is

A weight-loss plateau means your weight has stayed roughly the same for three to four weeks despite following your plan. A few days without change is not a plateau; it is normal fluctuation. Water retention from salt, a hard workout, a large meal, or hormonal changes can hide fat loss on the scale for a week or more. Before changing anything, check that the stall has lasted at least three weeks, and look at the average of several weigh-ins rather than a single reading. Many supposed plateaus disappear on their own once the water shifts.

Why plateaus happen

Plateaus happen for predictable reasons. As you lose weight, your body needs fewer calories, so the deficit that worked at 90 kg may be too small at 80 kg. You may also be moving less without noticing, because a lighter body burns fewer calories doing the same activity, and because dieting can quietly reduce spontaneous movement. Food tracking often drifts too: portions creep up, snacks go unrecorded, and cooking oil is forgotten. None of this is failure. It is the normal result of a body adapting and a routine settling in, and it can be corrected with small adjustments.

First, check the basics

Before cutting calories further, audit your current plan honestly. Track everything you eat and drink for a week, including sauces, drinks, and bites while cooking. Check that your calorie target still fits your new weight using the calculator on our home page. Weigh yourself under the same conditions, such as first thing in the morning after using the bathroom. Look at other measures too: waist size, how clothes fit, energy, and strength. If your waist is shrinking while the scale is flat, you may be losing fat and gaining or keeping muscle, which is progress the scale hides.

Adjusting food and movement

If the audit shows the plan is accurate and the stall is real, make one small change at a time. Cut 100 to 200 calories a day, or add 2,000 steps, or add one extra strength session a week. Do not do all three at once, because then you cannot tell what worked. Keep the change for two to three weeks before judging it. Eating more protein and fibre helps with hunger during a cut. Avoid dropping calories sharply; a large cut usually brings fatigue and rebound eating. Small, patient adjustments restart loss more reliably than dramatic ones.

Diet breaks and refeeding

After months of dieting, a planned diet break can help. A diet break means eating at maintenance calories for one or two weeks, not eating freely. This can reduce hunger hormones, restore energy, and make the next phase of loss easier. Some people also use a single higher-calorie day, but a full break is more effective for most. Plan the break in advance, keep tracking loosely, and return to the deficit on a set date. A break is not giving up; it is a deliberate part of a long plan, and people who use them often reach their goal more sustainably.

Mindset and the long view

Plateaus test patience more than method. Weight loss is not a straight line; it is a downward trend with flat stretches and small rises. People who succeed long term are the ones who keep the habits during the flat stretches instead of abandoning them. Review your reasons for losing weight, celebrate non-scale progress such as better fitness and sleep, and ask for support if you feel stuck. If a plateau lasts more than six to eight weeks despite honest tracking and adjustments, or if you feel unwell, speak to your doctor or a registered dietitian.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should a plateau last before I change my plan?

Wait three to four weeks of no change before adjusting. Shorter stalls are usually water fluctuation, not a real plateau.

Should I eat less to break a plateau?

Only a little. Cut 100 to 200 calories a day, or add movement instead. Large cuts often backfire through hunger and fatigue.

What is a diet break?

A planned one to two weeks at maintenance calories, after months of dieting. It can reduce hunger and fatigue and make the next phase of weight loss easier.
